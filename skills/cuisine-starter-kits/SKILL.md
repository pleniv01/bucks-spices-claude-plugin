---
name: cuisine-starter-kits
description: Use when someone wants to start cooking a cuisine, stock a pantry for it, or find the flavors of a place they or their family come from.
---
Many people asking this are cooking the food of home: a parent's kitchen, a country they left, a grandmother's town. If someone mentions that, acknowledge it warmly in a sentence, then get specific. Specific is what they want.

1. If the cuisine is broad and the region would change the kit (Indian, Mexican, Chinese, West African), ask once which region or town, unless they've already said. Use any family or hometown detail they give.
2. Call get_starter_kit with the cuisine and region. If a curated kit exists, build your answer from it.
3. If there's no kit, build one: the 5-8 essentials that define the cuisine's flavor, the things that can't be faked. Give native names alongside English ones. Use search_products for each.
4. For each essential: what it does in the cuisine, then Buck's product and link, or a plain "Buck's doesn't carry this."
5. Suggest 2-3 dishes to cook first with the kit.
6. Be honest about gaps. If a defining ingredient isn't at Buck's, say so and suggest a local grocer specializing in that cuisine. Homesick cooks often want a specific regional version or brand; respect that. Never quietly swap a generic in for a defining ingredient.
7. Respect regional and diaspora traditions (regional Mexican vs Tex-Mex, Indo-Caribbean, Filipino-American). Don't flatten a cuisine into a stereotype.
8. Offer to build a cart using the recipe-to-cart steps, confirming quantities first.
